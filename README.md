# 🌱 DIGITAL GREENWALL
### From Empathy to Execution: Designing a User-Centred AI Digital Solution

## About the Project

Digital GreenWall is a gamified sustainability application designed to encourage young people to reconnect with nature and participate more consistently in environmental activities.

The idea behind the project was to explore how digital technology, rather than simply competing for young people's attention, could be used to encourage meaningful environmental action. Through daily eco-challenges, virtual gardening, progress tracking, team missions and rewards, Digital GreenWall aims to make sustainability more engaging and accessible.

**Explore the application:** [Digital GreenWall – Interactive Prototype](https://hoodie-piano-59755917.figma.site/)

---

## The Design Thinking Process

The project was developed through five stages, moving from understanding the problem to designing, building and improving a digital solution.

### Stage 1 – Empathize and Define

I began by investigating the relationship between young people's digital lifestyles, connection with nature and environmental participation. I conducted a Google Forms survey and reviewed existing research on nature connectedness, environmental awareness and gamification.

The findings helped me identify a gap between environmental awareness and consistent participation. This became the foundation of the Digital GreenWall concept.

### Stage 2 – Ideate and Sketch

Using the SCAMPER technique and a How Might We question, I explored ways to make environmental activities more engaging, rewarding and social.

I developed the initial concepts for the Daily Eco-Challenge Board and Real-world Eco-Mission Tracker, along with the user flow and design principles of engagement, progression and community.

### Stage 3 – Prototype and Build

I used Figma Make to transform the initial ideas into an interactive application prototype. Through prompt-based development and iterative refinement, I created the different screens and connected their interactions.

The prototype brought together daily challenges, a virtual garden, progress tracking, team missions, rewards and an evolving eco avatar.

### Stage 4 – AI Logic Simulation and Adaptive Features

I explored how the application could respond to individual user behaviour through adaptive recommendations.

The proposed logic considers activity preferences, completed challenges and participation frequency to suggest suitable activities, reminders and team missions. This stage focused on designing and simulating the decision-making process rather than training a live AI model.

### Stage 5 – Testing, Reflection and Improvement

The final stage focused on identifying ways to make Digital GreenWall more useful beyond its existing digital features.

Two additional features were developed: the **Green Rewards Marketplace**, connecting eco-points with practical incentives, and **Green Around You**, helping users discover environmental activities within their communities.

These additions strengthened the connection between digital engagement and real-world environmental participation.

## Reflection: My Learning Throughout the Process

Working on Digital GreenWall helped me understand that developing an application involves much more than coming up with an idea and designing an interface. It requires understanding the people who will use it, identifying an actual problem and continuously questioning whether the proposed solution addresses their needs.

One of my biggest learnings was the importance of research before development. The primary and secondary research helped me move beyond my initial assumptions and consider the factors that influence environmental participation. It also showed me that a solution should be based on user needs rather than simply on features that seem interesting to build.

The prototyping process introduced me to prompt-based application development through Figma Make. While it helped accelerate the development process, I realised that generated outputs still require careful review, testing and refinement. I had to identify missing features, improve interactions and make sure the different parts of the application worked together.

I also gained a better understanding of how AI-driven systems can use patterns in user behaviour to provide personalised experiences. Although my current adaptive logic uses predefined rules, designing the decision-making process helped me understand how a more advanced system could eventually work.

Perhaps the most important lesson was that engagement alone is not enough. An application can have attractive interfaces, points and rewards, but its real value depends on whether it encourages meaningful action beyond the screen.

## Conclusion

Digital GreenWall began as an exploration of the disconnect between young people's digital lifestyles and their engagement with nature. Through research, ideation, prototyping and refinement, it developed into a concept that combines digital interaction with real-world environmental participation.

The project demonstrated how design thinking and emerging AI tools can be brought together to develop a user-centred digital solution. While the current application is a prototype with limitations, it provides a foundation for future development involving real environmental partnerships, verified local events, persistent user data and more advanced personalised recommendations.

Ultimately, Digital GreenWall is built around a simple idea: **small actions, when made engaging and consistent, can contribute to a larger environmental impact.**
